Re: Topological Semantics for Scoped Computational Paths

Dear Editors,

Please consider the manuscript, "Topological Semantics for Scoped Computational
Paths," for publication in the Logic Journal of the IGPL. It studies how
quotient topologies retain information in explicit equality witnesses after
the rewrites declared by a computational presentation.

The paper compares a topology that retains the full signed word with an
observable topology that retains endpoints, length and exact geometric
realization. A three-label circle proves that equal-slot and recursively timed
binary realizations can yield nonhomeomorphic observable based quotients.
Continuous variable-length word substitutions, preserving named rules and
inverse primitive witnesses up to scoped rewriting, yield full-word quotient
homeomorphisms at fixed endpoints, globally and on final composable-pair
domains. A conservative generator abbreviation gives a complementary failure:
the algebraic presentation equivalence survives, but its canonical observable
inverse is discontinuous. The claim concerns that inverse, without excluding
every unrelated bijection.

Composition is continuous on the final quotient of composable representatives.
Four equivalent conditions characterize agreement with the ordinary pair
topology and imply ordinary continuity. A published path-class open-map theorem
gives the universal positive case under local path-connectedness and semilocal
simple connectivity. Exact circle and torus presentations have checked normal
forms and geometric completeness, with primitive interchange supporting
products. For the two-circle rose, an interval-glued Cayley covering and a
jointly continuous contraction classify all continuous based loops by reduced
words. The ordinary fundamental-group comparison is algebraic; the paper
separately proves discreteness of the computational based quotients.

The abstract complete discrete-label separation mechanism is checked under an
explicit nondiscreteness hypothesis. Harmonic-archipelago geometry and the
stated Hawaiian-earring facts remain external inputs. Classical geometric
results and the published universal theorem are credited, and the paper
separates its present claims from the narrower Palomar registration.

The working manuscript has 35 pages including references and review-copy alt
text, with eleven explanatory figures; the technical supplement has 18 pages.
